# Blueprint: Your First Melody — "Mary Had a Little Lamb", Right Hand, Steady

Working document for the author. Not shown to the learner.

## Brief

- **Learner:** an adult or teenager who has never played any keyboard instrument and does not read music. Has a 61-key home keyboard. Learning alone (no teacher).
- **Destination:** play "Mary Had a Little Lamb" (8 bars) with the right hand, correct notes and fingers, with a metronome at ♩ = 60, three times in a row without stopping.
- **Scope:**
  - *In:* sitting and hand shape; safety; finding white keys by the black-key groups; middle C; finger numbers; the C five-finger position; repeated notes and moving between keys; pulse, counting, bars, quarter/half/whole notes; tempo and metronome; a short-loop practice method; self-checks; moving the same tune to G position (transfer).
  - *Out:* reading staff notation, left hand, hands together, black-key notes in a melody, scales with thumb crossings, dotted rhythms, pedals, dynamics (except an optional aside).
- **Format:** Markdown. A notebook adds nothing for a physical skill (skill rule for piano).
- **Assumptions made without asking:**
  1. The keyboard is a standard 61-key instrument whose lowest and highest keys are both C (C2–C7). The lesson tells the learner how to check this with the black-key rule and what to do if not.
  2. The learner can get a metronome: built into the keyboard, a free phone app, or a website.
  3. The learner knows the tune "Mary Had a Little Lamb" by ear, or can find a recording. The lesson does not depend on this; the notes and lengths are fully written out.
  4. "Steadily" means a steady beat at a chosen tempo, not a fast tempo. ♩ = 60 is chosen as a realistic first target.
  5. No physical limitations; the lesson still includes stop-if-it-hurts guidance.
  6. No staff notation: the skill says to describe notes with letter names and rhythm values when correct staff images cannot be produced; it also keeps the lesson focused.
- **Sources read:**
  - Wikipedia, "Mary Had a Little Lamb" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Had_a_Little_Lamb (poem by Sarah Josepha Hale, published 1830; today sung to the tune of "Merrily We Roll Along").
  - Wikipedia, "Goodnight, Ladies" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodnight,_Ladies (song as known today first published 16 May 1867; chorus "Merrily We Roll Along" has the same melody as "Mary Had a Little Lamb"), and its public-domain sheet-music image https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:38_Good-Night,_Ladies.png (used to verify the melody's pitches).
  - Benchoom, "Mary Had a Little Lamb Easy Piano" — https://benchoom.com/free-piano-sheets/mary-had-a-little-lamb/ (modern arrangement in C with fingering; used only to cross-check notes, rhythm values and fingers; not reproduced).
  - Skoove, "How to play Mary Had A Little Lamb on piano" — https://www.skoove.com/blog/mary-had-a-little-lamb-piano-tutorial/ (cross-check of the opening notes and fingers).
  - Soloist, "How to Find Middle C on Any Keyboard" — https://playsoloist.com/piano/middle-c (61-key: middle C is the third C from the left; transpose/octave buttons).
  - Wikipedia, "Fingering (music)" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingering_(music) (thumb = 1 … little finger = 5).
  - Wikipedia, "Tempo" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempo (60 bpm = one beat per second; in 4/4 the beat is a quarter note).
  - Wikipedia, "Note value" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Note_value (whole/half/quarter relationships).

## Central question

How does someone who has never played get from a silent keyboard to playing a whole melody with the right hand, on the right keys, with a beat steady enough that it never stops or stumbles?

**Motivating problem:** the learner wants to sit down and play "Mary Had a Little Lamb" all the way through, steadily. Right now they cannot find the notes, do not know which finger to use, and would naturally stop or slow down at every uncertain moment. The tune is the running example from the first paragraph to the final test.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the learner can:

1. Find any C, D, E, F, G, A or B on their keyboard using the groups of black keys, including middle C, and place the right hand in C position (thumb on middle C).
2. Sit at a suitable height with a relaxed, curved hand and level wrist, and recognise and release tension (or stop if something hurts).
3. Count a steady beat aloud and give quarter, half and whole notes their full 1, 2 and 4 beats.
4. Play "Mary Had a Little Lamb" with the right hand, correct notes and fingers, with a metronome at ♩ = 60, three times in a row without stopping.
5. Fix a trouble spot with a short-loop routine (isolate, slow down, count, set a number of clean repetitions, rejoin) and judge their own playing against stated self-checks.

## Misconceptions

| Misconception | Where it arises | How the lesson confronts it |
|---|---|---|
| Pressing harder (or squeezing a held key) makes a better or longer sound | §2, first key press | Explain the key reaches the bottom and the sound is already decided by how fast it went down; squeezing only adds tension. Test: press a key then squeeze — nothing changes |
| Each letter always has its own finger (E is "finger 3") | §4, C position | The finger is decided by where the hand is placed, not by the letter; previewed in §4, tested in E7 (in G position, finger 3 plays B) |
| "Steady" means playing each note when I'm ready (pausing before tricky notes, hurrying easy ones) | §5, the beat | Clock analogy and its limit; show that a pause before bar 4's G changes the rhythm; fix: pick a tempo slow enough for the hardest spot |
| A long note is finished once it has sounded (letting go of half/whole notes early) | §5, note lengths | Count "1 2" with the key held; check that the finger rises exactly as the next count arrives |
| Practising means playing from the start every time and stopping to fix mistakes | §6, practice method | Explain that this rehearses the opening most and the trouble spot least, and rehearses stopping; replace with short loops |
| Increasing speed is how you get steadier | §7, tempo ladder | Only move up after three clean repetitions; drop back when it breaks |

## Correctness risks

- Melody notes, lengths, and fingers for all 8 bars (verify against a public-domain source and an independent modern score).
- Public-domain status: poem 1830 (Hale), tune 1867 ("Goodnight, Ladies" / "Merrily We Roll Along"). Do not reproduce any modern arrangement.
- Black-key landmarks: C left of the 2-group; D between; E right; F left of the 3-group; G, A between; B right.
- 61-key range C2–C7: six Cs, five each of D–B; middle C = third C from the left; centre key is F♯.
- Finger numbers thumb = 1 … little finger = 5 (both hands).
- ♩ = 60 = one beat per second; quarter = 1 beat, half = 2, whole = 4 in 4/4; 8 bars × 4 beats = 32 beats ≈ 32 s.
- G position transposition: C→G, D→A, E→B, G→D; all white keys.
- Touch sensitivity claim: on a velocity-sensitive keyboard, loudness is set by key speed on the way down; extra pressure at the bottom does not change the sound on most home keyboards (some have aftertouch — hedge).
- Repeated notes need the key to rise before re-pressing.
